# The challenge

Open to any solver, offered in a friendly spirit. The aim is to separate two things that a single benchmark table cannot: finding known structure quickly, and solving problems faster in general. Both are worth doing. Only the second is a new kind of solver.

## Rules for every tier

1. **Certificates.** For a satisfiable instance, give the satisfying assignment. For an unsatisfiable one, give a proof in DRAT or LRAT format that `drat-trim` or `cake_lpr` accepts. An answer without a certificate does not count.
2. **Same machine.** Run your solver and Kissat (a stated release, default options) on the same hardware, one core each, and report both.
3. **Report everything.** Every instance in the tier, including timeouts and wrong answers. State the time limit.
4. **Say what the solver was given.** The DIMACS file only, or also the original circuit or graph.

A results file with one line per instance is enough: instance, answer, seconds, certificate file, and the same for Kissat.

## Tier 1: our 47 instances

Generated by `shortcuts/bench.py` and `shortcuts/more.py`; the files are in `instances/tier1/`, with checksums in `results/MANIFEST-tier1.sha256`.

What it shows: that your solver finds the same structure the known shortcuts use. Matching our times here is expected of any structure-aware solver and is not evidence of a new technique.

## Tier 2: a neutral public set

The main-track benchmark set of the most recent SAT Competition, as published by the organisers at [satcompetition.org](https://satcompetition.org/), with the competition's time limit of 5,000 seconds and its PAR-2 score (time for solved instances, twice the limit for unsolved ones).

If the whole set is too large, use the first 100 instances in order of their published hash, and say so.

What it shows: performance on problems nobody selected to suit a method.

## Tier 3: no known shortcut

25 uniform random 3-SAT instances at clause-to-variable ratio 4.26, with 300 to 500 variables and seeds 1 to 5, generated by `shortcuts/tier3.py`; checksums in `results/MANIFEST-tier3.sha256`. We have not solved the larger ones ourselves. Roughly half should be unsatisfiable, and those are hard for every known method.

The files are in `instances/tier3/`. The satisfiable ones are a known exception: a textbook local search solves all 15 in milliseconds (`results/TIER3-LOCALSEARCH.md`), so they count only as a sanity check. The unsatisfiable ones are the test.

What it shows: whether a speedup survives when there is no structure to find.

## What would count as new

We suggest, and others may reasonably disagree:

- **Strong evidence:** a PAR-2 score at least ten times better than Kissat's on Tier 2, or solving the unsatisfiable Tier 3 instances at 400 variables and above with checked proofs in a small fraction of Kissat's time.
- **Not evidence:** any result on Tier 1 alone, or on instance families defined only by the solver's author.

## Submitting

Open an issue or a pull request with your results file, the certificates or a link to them, and the hardware. We will rerun what we can and add it to the results, whichever way it comes out.
