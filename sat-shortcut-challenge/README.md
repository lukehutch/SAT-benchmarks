## Problem Description

Designed by Keith Adler to separate two distinct phenomena in SAT solver evaluation—detecting known polynomial-time structure vs. accelerating unstructured general search—the challenge defines three tiers (full rules in [`CHALLENGE.md`](CHALLENGE.md)):

- **Tier 1 (47 structured instances):** Generated by `src/bench.py` and `src/more.py` (`results/MANIFEST-tier1.sha256`), spanning Tseitin parity, PHP, adder/multiplier CEC miters, mutilated chessboards, Hamiltonian graphs, and XOR+clause formulas.
- **Tier 2 (neutral public corpus):** Main Track of the most recent SAT Competition (5,000 s PAR-2, or first 100 instances by published hash).
- **Tier 3 (25 unstructured uniform random 3-SAT instances — committed directly here):** Uniform random 3-SAT at the critical clause-to-variable phase transition ratio $m/n = 4.26$, across $n \in \{300, 350, 400, 450, 500\}$ variables and seeds $s \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$, generated by `src/tier3.py` and SHA-256 verified against [`results/MANIFEST-tier3.sha256`](results/MANIFEST-tier3.sha256). Roughly half are unsatisfiable, where no known structural shortcut applies.

## Published Upstream Baseline & Same-Machine Spot Check

### Upstream Tier-3 Baseline (`results/TIER3-BASELINE.md`)
- **Kissat 4.0.4 (default options, 300 s wall limit, 4 concurrent on 8-core Apple laptop):** Solved **17 / 25** (15 SAT, 2 UNSAT: `n300_s1` in 43.15 CPU s and `n300_s5` in 56.21 CPU s); 8 instances timed out past 300 s.
