## Problem Description

Designed by Keith Adler to separate two distinct phenomena in SAT solver evaluation—detecting known polynomial-time structure vs. accelerating unstructured general search—the challenge defines three tiers (full rules in [`CHALLENGE.md`](CHALLENGE.md)):

- **Tier 1 (`tier1/`, 47 instances across 9 families — committed directly here):** Generated by `src/bench.py` and `src/more.py` and SHA-256 verified against [`results/MANIFEST-tier1.sha256`](results/MANIFEST-tier1.sha256), spanning Tseitin parity, Pigeonhole Principle, uniform Random 3-SAT, Ripple-Carry vs. Kogge–Stone adder CEC miters, Array vs. Wallace multiplier CEC miters, multiplier target inversion, mutilated chessboards, Hamiltonian cycles (planted, unbalanced bipartite, cut-vertex), and XOR systems with random 3-clauses.
- **Tier 2 (neutral public corpus):** Main Track of the most recent SAT Competition (5,000 s PAR-2, or first 100 instances by published hash) — see [`../sat-competition-2024-2026-main-track/`](../sat-competition-2024-2026-main-track/README.md) (1,166 unique Main Track CNFs across 2024–2026).
- **Tier 3 (`tier3/`, 25 unstructured uniform random 3-SAT instances — committed directly here):** Uniform random 3-SAT at the critical clause-to-variable phase transition ratio $m/n = 4.26$, across $n \in \{300, 350, 400, 450, 500\}$ variables and seeds $s \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$, generated by `src/tier3.py` and SHA-256 verified against [`results/MANIFEST-tier3.sha256`](results/MANIFEST-tier3.sha256). Roughly half are unsatisfiable, where no known structural shortcut applies.
